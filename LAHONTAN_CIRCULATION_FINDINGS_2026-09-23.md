## KEY INSIGHT: THE "FORGOTTEN BUT REVIEWED" PARADOX

**The Problem:** Thwaites (1905) and earlier sources claim Lahontan was "copiously copied through 1740s, then all but forgotten" after 1744's exposure of the Long River fabrication. Yet American evidence contradicts this:

| Evidence | Date | Status |
|----------|------|--------|
| Peak European circulation | 1703-1740 | 25+ editions |
| Exposure/disrepute begins | 1744 | Charlevoix & Bellin |
| Library Company holding | 1789 | Confirmed (page 92) |
| **Major American review** | **1816** | North American Review (Smith age 11) |
| French Canadian dismissal | 1744-1863 | "liar and traitor" |

**Interpretation Options:**
1. **European vs. American divergence**: Lahontan forgotten in European/French scholarship but sustained American interest
2. **Rediscovery/revival**: American rediscovery of a genuinely obscure text circa 1810s
3. **Thwaites/secondary overstatement**: "Forgotten" applies to elite scholarly circles, not libraries/general educated public
4. **Carver intermediary**: Lahontan's ideas transmitted via Carver (1778, 23+ editions) rather than direct reading

### JOSEPH SMITH EXPOSURE: The Critical Path

**Confirmed pathways:**
- Library Company of Philadelphia held Lahontan (1789), but not Manchester library (Paul 1982) where Smith spent childhood
- North American Review actively reviewed Lahontan (1816), but unclear if it reached Palmyra, NY
- Jonathan Carver's *Travels* (1778, 23+ editions through 1830s) demonstrably borrowed from Lahontan and circulated widely

**Unresolved:**
- Did NAR subscriptions reach Palmyra institutions?
- Which Carver editions included the Lahontan passage?
- Did Pittsburgh institutions (post-1822 Rigdon) hold Lahontan?
- Are there additional 1820s-1860s periodical mentions undocumented in secondary literature?

### Next Inference for Dossier Update
The 1816 NAR review is the single strongest evidence of active American intellectual engagement with Lahontan during Smith's formative era (Smith was 11). However, this **does not establish Smith's personal exposure** — only that educated Americans were discussing Lahontan when Smith was a teenager. The availability argument still requires: (1) proof that NAR reached non-subscription audiences in NY/PA, OR (2) documentation of Lahontan circulation through Pittsburgh institutions post-1822 (Rigdon pathway), OR (3) confirmation that Carver's borrowings from Lahontan were substantial enough to constitute indirect transmission.

## APPENDIX: AHONE/LIAHONA PHONETIC CONNECTION (Resolved)

**Question:** Could "Ahone" (Powhatan deity from Virginia records) be a source for "Liahona"?

**Investigation:** Checked whether Robert Beverley's 1705 *History of Virginia* independently attests "Ahone" as an earlier published source than Strachey's manuscript.

**Findings:**
- **Beverley 1705**: Mentions Okee (7×), Quioccos, Kiwasa — but **NO mention of "Ahone"**
- **Strachey manuscript** (~1610-1615, written): Contains Ahone reference
- **Strachey first published**: 1849 via Hakluyt Society edition
- **Smith/Book of Mormon**: 1830

**Verdict:** 
- Ahone was NOT available in any published source accessible to Joseph Smith (1805-1830)
- First print appearance: 1849 (19 years post-BoM publication)
- Phonetic similarity (Ahone/Liahona) is coincidental unless Smith had access to Strachey's unpublished manuscript
- **No evidential pathway established**
