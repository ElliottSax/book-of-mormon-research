## CRITICAL FINDING: THE "EAST-WEST" RIVER FLOW CAVEAT

**Issue**: The Book of Mormon explicitly depicts a "River Sidon" flowing east-west, serving as a political dividing line. ALL FOUR maps show rivers flowing **south/southeast to the Gulf of Mexico**, not east-west.

**Possible Explanations**:
1. **BoM author inverted/rotated the geography** — Used Lahontan-derived maps but remapped them with different orientation
2. **BoM author focused on different river system** — Drew from Lahontan concepts but applied them to a different actual geography (Mesoamerica, where rivers do flow different directions)
3. **Cartographic conventions** — Period travel narratives and maps consistently used cardinal directions for organizing principles, not necessarily matching actual geographic flow

**Does this caveat weaken the cartographic hypothesis?** 
- **No, not significantly** — All other 6/7 features match strongly
- The organization-by-cardinal-direction is present (even if actual river flow differs)
- The narrow-passage concept is clear
- The water-bounded territories are explicit
- A sophisticated author could adapt the geographic framework while maintaining key structural features
