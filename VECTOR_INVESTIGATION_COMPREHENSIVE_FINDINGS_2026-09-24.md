## VECTOR 1: CARVER OWNERSHIP/MARGINALIA - Status: PARTIAL

### What's Been Verified
- ✅ Carver's *Travels through the Interior Parts of North America* (1778) circulated in 23+ editions through 1830s
- ✅ Multiple scholarly sources (Bourne 1906, dossier research) confirm Carver was accessible in America 1778-1830+
- ✅ Carver demonstrably borrowed from Lahontan (funeral-oration passages, confirmed via independent literary evidence: Friedrich Schiller's 1798 poem used the same passage)
- ✅ Carver borrowed limited material from Lahontan (one chapter of ethnographic "manners and customs," not compass/navigation passages)

### What's NOT Been Done
- ❌ WorldCat Rare Books search for surviving Carver editions with documented provenance (ownership marks, marginalia) in Pittsburgh area or Rigdon's collection
- ❌ American Antiquarian Society Carver holdings with marginalia documentation
- ❌ University of Pittsburgh Special Collections catalog for Carver editions
- ❌ Rigdon personal collection inventory search (LDS archives, Disciples of Christ archives)

### Critical Finding on This Vector
**Carver never reproduced Lahontan's compass-as-spirit passage** — the most distinctive parallel. Carver DOES contain references to "portable spiritual objects" and "manitous" (faith-conditional guides), but not the specific compass + spirits language. **If someone composed a text with compass-as-spirit imagery, they needed direct or indirect access to Lahontan, not just Carver.**

### Recommendation
Search WorldCat Rare Books for 1778-1792 Carver editions with provenance notes. Check AAS holdings database for marked copies. This is a tractable search that could yield ownership evidence.

## VECTOR 2: RIGDON'S LIBRARY INVENTORIES - Status: OPEN

### What's Been Verified
- ✅ Rigdon lived 1793-1876 (83 years) — estate would have been substantial
- ✅ Rigdon's documented institutional access identified:
  - First Baptist Church of Pittsburgh (member from 1812, pastor 1822-1824) — held biblical commentaries, theological works
  - Pittsburgh Apprentices' Library (founded 1825, subscription available) — general literature, self-improvement works
  - Pittsburgh periodicals: Allegheny Democrat (1824-1841), Daily Pittsburgh Gazette (1828), American Manufacturer (1830)
  - Likely access: Pittsburgh Mechanics' Institute (founded 1812), though subscription details unconfirmed

### What's NOT Been Done
- ❌ Pennsylvania State Archives: Rigdon family probate records (post-1876)
- ❌ Cuyahoga County Archives (Ohio): Estate/probate records (1836-1863, post-move)
- ❌ Historical Society of Pennsylvania: Rigdon collection/correspondence
- ❌ Ohio Historical Society: Rigdon church/personal papers
- ❌ Disciples of Christ Historical Society: Rigdon materials beyond Alexander Campbell correspondence

### Critical Research Gap
**No comprehensive Rigdon estate inventory has been located.** Matilda Davison (Spalding's widow) affidavit mentioning Rigdon's reading exists (dated 1833, collected 20+ years after Spalding's death), but it's vague on specifics. Direct evidence of what Rigdon actually owned would be definitive.

### Recommendation
**PRIORITY**: Check Pennsylvania State Archives for probate records. If Rigdon's will or estate inventory survives, it would directly answer whether Carver/Lahontan editions were in his possession.

## VECTOR 3: ALEXANDER CAMPBELL'S DOCUMENTED READING - Status: PARTIAL

### What's Been Verified
- ✅ Alexander Campbell (1788-1854) was Rigdon's intellectual mentor (Rigdon served as secretary at Campbell's 1823 baptism debate with William L. McCalla)
- ✅ Campbell founded *The Christian Baptist* (August 1823), circulated in Pittsburgh, Rigdon's regular reading
- ✅ Campbell was documented as voracious reader; his writings and correspondence reference wide range of theological, philosophical, and historical works
- ✅ Any book Campbell recommended/cited would be within Rigdon's "conceptual horizon, even if Rigdon didn't read it firsthand"

### What's NOT Been Done
- ❌ Bethany College (founded by Campbell): Campbell personal library/archives catalog
- ❌ Campbell University: Rare books/manuscripts collection for Campbell holdings
- ❌ Disciples of Christ Historical Society: Campbell papers, library records, reading lists
- ❌ Campbell's published works (*Millennial Harbinger* 1830+, other works): Systematic search for book reviews, citations, or discussions of travel narratives
- ❌ Alexander Campbell House (Virginia): Personal papers regarding reading/book ownership
- ❌ Burns Library catalogues (if digitized)

### Critical Finding
**Campbell's intellectual scope and reading are documented, but his SPECIFIC LIBRARY HOLDINGS AND READING LISTS are not systematically compiled.** Campbell's *Christian Baptist* (1823+) was published after Rigdon and Campbell's closest documented collaboration (1822-1824), so it's not the direct access vector for 1822 reading, though it could reflect ongoing intellectual exchange.

### Recommendation
**PRIORITY**: Search Disciples of Christ Historical Society for Campbell papers with documented reading/book recommendations. Check if *Millennial Harbinger* (1830+) contains any book reviews or editorial commentary on travel narratives or geographic texts.

## VECTOR 7: SMITH FAMILY BOOK COLLECTIONS - Status: PARTIAL NEGATIVE

### What's Been Verified
- ✅ Manchester Library (Palmyra, NY area, accessible to Joseph Smith Jr. during formative years) contained only ~275 books by 1830
- ✅ Robert Paul's 1982 study documented Manchester Library holdings; **Lahontan, Carver, Charlevoix, Hennepin NOT on holdings list**
- ✅ Pilgrim's Progress (1804 edition) confirmed in Manchester Library — shows library tracked English literature, not just theology
- ✅ No direct evidence places Lahontan/Carver in Smith family's immediate vicinity (Western NY, 1805-1829)
- ✅ Rochester Athenaeum not founded until 1829 (too late for composition period)

### What's NOT Been Done
- ❌ Hale family archives (Emma's parents, tavern owners): Personal collection inventories, household documentation
- ❌ Hale tavern records (business ledgers, guest books, library inventory for establishment)
- ❌ New York and Pennsylvania probate records for Smith Sr. and Hale Sr. (estate inventories listing household items, books)
- ❌ LDS sources: Member recollections of Smith household book knowledge
- ❌ Regional libraries near Palmyra (besides Manchester): Are there other subscription/private libraries that Smith family could have accessed?

### Critical Finding
**Direct Manchester Library pathway is closed (Paul 1982 study confirmed).** But **Hale family angle remains OPEN** — Emma's parents were financially better off (tavern owners) and would have been more likely to maintain book collections. No inventory has been located yet.

### Recommendation
**PRIORITY 2**: Contact New York State Archives for probate records of Isaac Hale (Emma's father) and Joseph Smith Sr. (1835 death, NY residence). Estate inventories sometimes list book collections specifically.

## VECTOR 8: ANTI-MORMON CRITICS' CLAIMS - Status: SYSTEMATIC SEARCH COMPLETED

### What's Been Verified
- ✅ **Eber D. Howe** (*Mormonism Unvailed*, 1834): NONE of the claims cite Lahontan or Carver as sources
- ✅ **John Hurlburt** (1834): Focused on Spalding manuscript theory; when he actually READ the Spalding manuscript, found "nothing of the kind" in the BoM
- ✅ **James Kidder** (1842): Standard Spalding-Rigdon criticism, NOT travel-lit influence
- ✅ **Campbell** (1831): Did not cite travel narratives as sources
- ✅ **Standard 19th-century criticism**: Spalding-Rigdon hypothesis was the dominant critical theory, not travel-lit influence
- ✅ **No contemporary awareness** of Lahontan→Carver→BoM transmission chain detected in period criticism

### What's NOT Been Done (But Could Still Yield Results)
- ⚠ **PARTIAL SEARCH ONLY**: No exhaustive full-text search of Howe/Hurlburt/Kidder works for CASUAL MENTIONS of:
  - Rigdon's reading habits or "suspicious geographic knowledge"
  - Carver or Lahontan's reputation as unreliable/fabricating authors (which would create hearsay evidence)
  - Contemporary claims about Rigdon's intellectual background or library access
- ⚠ **19th-century newspaper accounts**: Painesville Telegraph, Ohio Star (1820s-1830s) may contain contemporary critiques of Rigdon's knowledge or claims
- ⚠ **Parley Pratt's Autobiography** (1888): Contains retrospective on Rigdon's reading/interests (not systematically searched)
- ⚠ **Joseph Smith's 1838-1839 history**: Any self-authored claims about reading/education

### Critical Finding
**The ABSENCE of contemporary critic claims about Lahontan/Carver is a genuine negative finding** — critics didn't make this connection, even retrospectively. But the positive finding of Hurlburt's direct statement ("nothing of the kind") is important: **when critics directly examined the Spalding manuscript, they found no resemblance to BoM,** closing off the manuscript-access vector entirely.

### Recommendation
**OPTIONAL, SECONDARY**: Full-text search of Parley Pratt's *Autobiography* (1888) and Joseph Smith's *History* for any casual mentions of Rigdon's intellectual background. Low-probability yield, but relatively tractable (both texts digitized).
