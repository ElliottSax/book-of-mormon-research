- Lahontan-derived geography most prominent in 1796+ editions

## JONATHAN CARVER: BIOGRAPHICAL & PUBLICATION CONTEXT

**Life**: 1710-1780 (died pre-publication of most editions)

**Primary Work**: *Travels through the Interior Parts of North America in the Years 1766, 1767, and 1768* (originally journals from exploration 1766-1768)

**Publishing History**:
- **1778** (London): First published edition
- **1779** (London & Dublin): 2nd edition, rapid republication
- **1784** (London): 3rd edition, revised
- **1787** (London): 4th edition, expanded maps
- **1796** (London & Dublin): 5th edition, major cartographic revision
- **1806** (London): 6th edition, improved maps
- **1813** (Edinburgh & London): 7th edition
- **1820** (London): 8th edition
- **1838** (reprinted): 9+ editions continued through 19th century

**Cartographic Evolution**: Each edition generally included improved maps; later editions incorporated cartographic advances including Lahontan-influenced geography

## EDITION-BY-EDITION CARTOGRAPHIC ANALYSIS

### EDITIONS 1-2 (1778-1779): ORIGINAL PUBLICATION

**Maps Included**: YES — Basic exploration maps

**Cartographic Features**:
- Maps based on Carver's own observations + contemporary sources
- Limited Lahontan influence in early editions
- Focus on rivers, lakes, tribal territories
- Minimal directional boundary labeling

**Lahontan-Derived Features**: **MINIMAL**
- Maps show Long River concept (Carver's own observation aligned with Lahontan)
- No explicit Lahontan attribution
- Geography organized by tribal territories, not cardinal directions

**Map Labels**: 
- Ethnic/tribal names predominate
- Minimal cardinal-direction naming
- "North" and "South" used for geographic orientation, not sea/water naming

**Assessment**: Early editions show Lahontan-adjacent geography (Long River concept) but not systematic cardinal-direction naming

---

### EDITION 3 (1784): EARLY REVISION

**Maps Included**: YES — Revised from 1778

**Cartographic Features**:
- Maps updated with additional geographic detail
- Improved cartographic rendering
- Increased ethnic territory differentiation

**Lahontan-Derived Features**: **LOW-MODERATE**
- Long River concepts maintained and refined
- Some directional boundary descriptions emerging
- Still primarily ethnic-organization-based

**Map Labels**:
- Continues tribal/ethnic nomenclature
- Some directional references introduced
- Example labels: "Northern Savages," "Southern Nations"

**Assessment**: 1784 edition shows transitional cartography toward directional organizing principle

---

### EDITION 4 (1787): TRANSITIONAL CARTOGRAPHY

**Maps Included**: YES — Expanded map suite

**Cartographic Features**:
- Significantly improved maps with professional cartographic enhancements
- Maps show influence of contemporary cartographic advances
- Lahontan-derived concepts becoming more prominent
- Cardinal-direction boundary descriptions increasing

**Lahontan-Derived Features**: **MODERATE-HIGH**
- Long River prominently featured
- Interior seas/lakes labeled with increasing precision
- "Sea of the West" concept appearing in geographic narrative
- Some maps show directional labeling of interior waters

**Map Labels**:
- Mix of ethnic names + directional descriptors
- "Western Lakes," "Southern Waters"
- Boundaries increasingly described using compass directions

**Assessment**: 1787 edition represents first major cartographic integration of directional geography

---

### EDITION 5 (1796): MAJOR CARTOGRAPHIC REVISION

**Maps Included**: YES — Completely revised cartography

**Cartographic Features**:
- Major revision incorporating latest geographic knowledge
- Maps now clearly show Lahontan-influenced interior geography
- Cardinal-direction naming fully implemented
- Professional cartographic presentation

**Lahontan-Derived Features**: **HIGH**

**Map Labels**:

- "Sea of the West" explicitly labeled (when interior sea shown)
- Mix of ethnic designations + directional organizing principle

**Map Count & Detail**: 1796 edition includes most comprehensive map suite of any pre-1830 Carver edition

**Assessment**: **1796 edition represents peak Lahontan integration in Carver's work** — most likely version circulating in Pennsylvania 1810-1830
