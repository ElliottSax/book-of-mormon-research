## BoM GEOGRAPHIC REQUIREMENTS (Quick Recap)

1. River Sidon flows **E-W** (east to west, east-to-west direction)
2. River divides **east and west** regions (political/military boundary)
3. Narrow neck: ~1.5 days' journey, connects Land Northward to Land Southward
4. Narrow passage runs **N-S** (north-south axis)
5. Lands organized by cardinal directions (North/South/East/West)
6. Nearly surrounded by water (peninsula-like or archipelago-like)
7. Coastal settlement pattern transitioning inland

## CRITICAL COMPARISON FRAMEWORK

### How to Compare Maps in Standard Orientation

For each map (de l'Isle, Popple, Senex), evaluate:

1. **River Flow Direction**
   - Flows E-W (✓ matches BoM)
   - Flows N-S (✗ doesn't match BoM)
   - Multiple rivers in different directions (? ambiguous)

2. **Territorial Divisions**
   - Organized N-S (✓ matches BoM)
   - Organized E-W (✗ doesn't match BoM)
   - Multiple divisions in different directions (? ambiguous)

3. **Narrow Passage Geometry**
   - Runs N-S (✓ matches BoM)
   - Runs E-W (✗ doesn't match BoM)
   - Multiple passages in different directions (? ambiguous)

4. **Distinctiveness**
   - How many features align?
   - How specifically do they match (or diverge)?
   - Is this combination rare in 18th-century cartography?

5. **Overall Verdict**
   - **STRONG MATCH in standard orientation**: No rotation needed
   - **WEAK MATCH in standard orientation**: Rotation hypothesis becomes more plausible
   - **NO MATCH in standard orientation**: Rotation becomes necessary (if matches with rotation)

## HYPOTHESIS REFINEMENT

### Scenario 1: Another Map Matches in Standard Orientation
**If de l'Isle, Popple, or Senex map shows**:
- River flowing E-W (or multiple rivers with E-W component)
- Territories organized N-S
- Narrow passage N-S
- **In standard orientation (North at top)**

**Then**:
- ✓ BoM author could have used this map without rotation
- ✗ Rotation hypothesis becomes less necessary
- → **Conclusion**: Rotation was not required; standard orientation suffices

### Scenario 2: All Maps Match Better After Rotation
**If all tested maps (Moll, de l'Isle, Popple, Senex) show**:
- Poor match in standard orientation (river flows N-S, territories E-W)
- Better match after 90° CW rotation (river flows E-W, territories N-S)

**Then**:
- ? Suggests rotation might be intentional transformation by BoM author
- ? OR suggests BoM author drew from different source entirely
- ⚠ Makes coincidental matching less likely (all maps show same pattern?)
- → **Conclusion**: Rotation might be necessary; indicates possible cartographic influence

### Scenario 3: Maps Show Varied Patterns
**If different maps (Moll vs. de l'Isle vs. Popple) show**:
- Some match in standard, others better with rotation
- Different geographic features align for different maps

**Then**:
- ? Suggests BoM geography is complex or drawn from multiple sources
- ? OR suggests no single cartographic source fully explains BoM
- → **Conclusion**: Cartographic influence exists but no single "smoking gun" map
