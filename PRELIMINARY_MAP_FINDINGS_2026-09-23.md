## WHAT WE ALREADY KNOW FROM RESEARCH LOG

### 1. CARDINAL-DIRECTION SEA NAMING: KEY FINDING

**Original Source**: Lahontan (1703)
- Lahontan's prose text does NOT use cardinal-direction sea naming (verified null)
- BUT: Lahontan's **maps** were influential

**First Cartographic Implementation**: Guillaume de l'Isle (1718)
- *Carte de la Louisiane et du cours du Mississipi* (1718, 1720)
- Explicitly depicted **"Mer de l'Ouest"** (Sea of the West) concept
- **Based directly on Lahontan's geographic account**

**Critical Finding**: "Mer de l'Ouest" concept predates Lahontan
- Guillaume Delisle's manuscript maps (c. 1695-1700)
- First print appearance via Nolin (c. 1700)
- Delisle won plagiarism suit vs. Nolin in 1706
- **Implication**: The cardinal-direction naming was NOT Lahontan's original invention, but Lahontan's *descriptions* helped formalize and popularize it

### 2. WIDE CIRCULATION: MOLL'S MAPS (STRONGEST CANDIDATE)

**Hermann Moll**: *A Map of North America*
- **23+ editions documented** (1709-1711 through 1740s)
- Most widely circulated of all Lahontan-derived maps
- **Multiple variants** allow tracking feature evolution
- **British publisher (London)** → Likely imported to America
- **Scholarly consensus** (from agent research): Appears in subscription library catalogs, including Franklin's Library Company of Philadelphia

**Why Moll Matters**: 
- Volume of editions means multiple opportunities for American audience exposure
- Popular appeal (more than official French or prestigious British maps)
- Cartographic historians identify Moll as **primary diffuser** of Lahontan concepts in English-speaking world

### 3. FEATURE FORMALIZATION: DE L'ISLE INFLUENCE

**Guillaume de l'Isle** (1718, 1720):
- Official French cartography
- Formalized Lahontan's geographic concepts for scientific/governmental use
- Influenced later mapmakers (Popple, Senex, Chatelain)
- **Copied by**: Moll, Senex, Popple (established de l'Isle as cartographic standard)

### 4. DERIVED MAP ECOSYSTEM

**Who copied whom**:
- de l'Isle (1718) ← Lahontan (1703) + Delisle's pre-Lahontan concepts
- Moll (1709-1740s editions) ← Lahontan + subsequent refinements
- Popple (1733) ← Moll, de l'Isle, others (synthesis)
- Senex (1710s-1720s) ← de l'Isle primarily

**Result**: By 1730s, cardinal-direction sea naming (inherited from Lahontan) is standard cartographic convention in maps of North America

### 5. SCHOLARLY CONSENSUS (FROM AGENT RESEARCH)

**Key Points**:
- Lahontan's 1703 publication **directly influenced de l'Isle's maps**
- Moll's maps (7-8 editions, 1720-1750s) **incorporated "Long River" from Lahontan**
- Popple's 1733 map is **most comprehensive**, shows **debate between old/new cartographic concepts**
- **Circulation evidence**: Maps appear in subscription library catalogs (Franklin's Library Company)
- **"Mer de l'Ouest" peaked 1720-1740**, then declined as exploration provided counter-evidence

## PRELIMINARY HYPOTHESIS (BEFORE DETAILED MAP RESEARCH)

### Strongest Candidate: Hermann Moll's Maps (1709-1740s)

**Reasons**:
1. **Most widely circulated** (23+ editions)
2. **Popular appeal** (not just elite/scientific use like de l'Isle)
3. **Available in America** (subscription libraries documented)
4. **Long River concept incorporated** (Lahontan's geographic ideas)
5. **Timeline plausible** (circulated during Rigdon's formative period)

**If Moll's maps show**:
- Cardinal-direction seas (expected: yes)
- Narrow passages (expected: likely)
- Multiple territories (expected: yes)
- River flowing through center (expected: yes)
- Wilderness barriers (expected: likely)

**Then match to BoM = STRONG** (multiple features converging in single map = harder to explain as coincidence)

### Alternative Candidate: de l'Isle's Carte de la Louisiane (1718, 1720)

**Advantages**:
- Authoritative French cartography
- Direct Lahontan implementation (not derivative)
- Possibly in Franklin's Library Company

**Disadvantages**:
- Less popular circulation than Moll
- French publication (less American reach before 1800s)
- More elite/scientific than popular audience
