## CRITICAL EVIDENCE ASSESSMENT

### IF Comoros was NOT mentioned in American publications before 1830:
- Hypothesis weakened but not eliminated (casual knowledge could circulate)
- Transmission pathway becomes less plausible
- "Coincidence" explanation becomes more likely

### IF Comoros was mentioned in Morse's Geography (1805-1823):
- Hypothesis significantly strengthened
- Rigdon's exposure becomes plausible through standard education
- Available to educated Americans like Spalding

### IF Comoros was mentioned in period newspapers or other accessible texts:
- Hypothesis strongly supported
- Multiple transmission pathways possible
- "Casual encounter" becomes plausible

### IF "Moroni" was a rare name in English 1800-1830:
- Comoros capital naming becomes remarkable coincidence
- Probability analysis becomes important

## COMPARATIVE BASELINE: LAHONTAN

**For reference** (from previous research):
- Lahontan was known to English speakers (20+ editions published)
- Confirmed in American institutions (Library Company of Philadelphia, Jefferson's library)
- BUT: Absent from Manchester/Palmyra area, "all but forgotten" by 1800s
- Carver's *Travels* provided secondary transmission pathway

**Comoros will likely be**: Less documented than Lahontan, more obscure, but possibly mentioned in geography texts
