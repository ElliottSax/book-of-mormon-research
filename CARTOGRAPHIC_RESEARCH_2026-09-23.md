# Cartographic Conventions 1700-1830: Cardinal Direction Naming Research
**Date: September 23, 2026**
**Research Task: Establish prevalence and examples of "sea north," "sea south," "sea east," "sea west" naming on period maps and in geographic literature**

---

## RESEARCH QUESTIONS

1. **CARDINAL SEA NAMING**: Is naming seas by cardinal direction a standard cartographic convention?
2. **TEMPORAL EMERGENCE**: When did this convention appear? 16th c.? 17th c.? 18th c.?
3. **GEOGRAPHIC EXAMPLES**: Where on real maps do we see "sea north," "sea south," etc. as proper nouns?
4. **LAHONTAN's USAGE**: In Lahontan's New Voyages, did he use cardinal directions to name geographic features?
5. **CARVER's GEOGRAPHY**: How did Carver name his geographic features?
6. **GREAT LAKES NAMING**: Did colonial American maps use cardinal directions for the Great Lakes?
7. **SEA OF THE WEST**: How was the "Sea of the West" actually named and depicted on maps?
8. **EARLY AMERICAN PERIODICALS**: Did the North American Review or other literature use this convention?

## PRELIMINARY FINDINGS NEEDED

The following sources need direct examination to answer the research questions:

### Priority 1: Sea of the West Cartography

- **Hermann Moll maps** (early 18th century) — Similar question; Moll was major producer of maps incorporating Lahontan
- **Henry Popple's Map of North America** (1733) — How was the interior sea depicted?
- **John Senex maps** — Similar examination needed

### Priority 2: Lahontan's Original Accounts
- **Lahontan's New Voyages text** (1703, English edition) — Direct search for how Lahontan names geographic features
  - Does he use "sea east," "sea west," "sea north," "sea south"?
  - Or does he use proper nouns (e.g., "Tahuglauk's Lake," "Great Salt Water")?
  - How are cardinal directions incorporated into his naming?

### Priority 3: Carver's Travels
- **Carver's Travels through the Interior Parts of North America** (1778) — Examination of geographic naming conventions
  - Since Carver borrowed from Lahontan, how did he adapt the naming?
  - Does he use cardinal directions or inherit specific place-names?

### Priority 4: Colonial American Geographic Literature
- **Jedidiah Morse's American Universal Geography** (1805+) — Examination of how geographic features are named
- **Contemporary travel narratives** (1790-1830) — How do they name seas and bodies of water?
- **North American Review** (1816) review of Lahontan — What does the review say about his geographic claims?

### Priority 5: Hebrew Sea Naming
- **Robert Pate's analysis** ("Seas and Seashores in the Book of Mormon") — Already confirmed that Hebrew "yam" carries dual sense of "sea" and "west"
  - This explains "sea west" but leaves "sea north" and "sea south" unaccounted for
