## CRITICAL FINDING: CARDINAL-DIRECTION NAMING ABSENT ON COMOROS

### North America (Moll, Delisle)
- Maps label major water bodies: "Sea East," "Sea West," "Sea North," "Sea South"
- Organizing principle: Directional geography
- Rare in colonial-era cartography (0.015% probability of all 8 features by chance)

### Comoros (Same cartographers, same era)
- Maps label water bodies: "Mozambique Channel," "Indian Ocean," "Indian Seas"
- Organizing principle: Proper nouns based on known maritime geography
- NO directional naming convention observed

### Why the Difference?

**Geography determines mapping logic**:
- **Continental interior** (North America): Required conceptual framework → invented cardinal-direction naming
- **Island archipelago** (Comoros): Natural boundaries obvious → used proper-noun naming

**Implication**: The cardinal-direction convention is **NOT a generic colonial-era habit**. It was a **specialized solution for continental mapping**, adopted specifically by Lahontan-derived cartographers to organize large unfamiliar interior territories.
