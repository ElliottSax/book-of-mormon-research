   - "Sea south" (Helaman 3:8)

   - Function: Organizing principle for territories, not optional descriptors

2. **River Sidon**
   - Named as "River Sidon" (Alma 2:34, 4:4, 15:3, 16:6-7, 22:29, Helaman 3:3-4)
   - Frequency: 11+ explicit occurrences
   - Function: Territorial boundary (Nephites east, Lamanites west = Alma 22:27)

3. **Water as Boundary Feature**
   - Seas bound the entire territory on east and west (Alma 22:27)

4. **Unnamed Rivers / Water Systems**
   - "River flowing northward" (Alma 22:29-30, contextual: waters feeding northern wilderness)

## PART II: LAHONTAN-DERIVED MAPS — COMPLETE FEATURE INVENTORY

### A. WATER FEATURES ON MAPS (COMPLETE ANALYSIS)

#### 1. **Cardinal-Direction Sea Naming Convention**
- **de l'Isle (1718/20)**: 16-point compass rose; gulf/Atlantic labeled with directional references
- **Moll (1709-1740s)**: Strategic directional nomenclature; "Spanish holdings East," "British territories West"
- **Popple (1733)**: Multi-sheet administrative organization using cardinal directions
- **Senex (1710s-20s)**: Regional nomenclature with directional organization
- **Origin**: Lahontan (1703) introduced systematic directional organization; cartographers formalized it
- **Distinctiveness**: VERY HIGH — This convention is RARE in period cartography

#### 2. **River Systems as Central Organizing Features**
- **de l'Isle**: Mississippi as spine; tributaries marked
- **Moll**: Mississippi prominently displayed (multiple editions emphasize different aspects)
- **Popple**: Mississippi + Ohio explicitly shown; extensive tributary detail
- **Senex**: Delaware, Ohio, Mississippi all prominent; river hierarchy clear
- **Organization Principle**: Rivers divide territories; define settlement zones
- **Navigation**: Water routes marked as primary transportation

#### 3. **Water-Bounded Territories**
- **All four maps**: Territories shown surrounded by Atlantic, Gulf, rivers, lakes
- **Specificity**: Gulf of Mexico labeled; Atlantic Ocean labeled; internal water systems labeled
- **Political Function**: Water features define territorial boundaries between colonial holdings

#### 4. **Multiple Coastal Water Features**
- Bays (Delaware Bay, Chesapeake Bay, Mobile Bay, etc.)
- River mouths/deltas
- Coastal inlets
- **Pattern**: Coastal geography detailed extensively

---

### B. TERRITORIAL ORGANIZATION ON MAPS

#### 1. **Multi-Territorial Division**
- **de l'Isle**: French (west), British (north/east), Spanish (south) territories
- **Moll**: British, French, Spanish holdings; territorial contested/disputed zones
- **Popple**: British holdings subdivided; colonial administrative regions; Georgia expansion
- **Senex**: Colonial administrative boundaries; Pennsylvania, Maryland, Virginia, Carolina distinctions

#### 2. **North-South Progression Emphasis**
- **All maps**: North-south organizing axis primary; east-west secondary
- **Regional hierarchy**: Upper/Lower divisions repeated across all maps

#### 3. **Nested Regional Hierarchy**
- Tier 1: Major colonial holdings (France, Britain, Spain)
- Tier 2: Sub-regional territories (Louisiana, Carolina, Virginia, Pennsylvania)
- Tier 3: Local administrative/settlement zones
- **Pattern**: Tripartite or quaternary hierarchy consistent

### B. INTERPRETIVE NOTE ON PHONETIC MATCHING

**Finding**: The absence of phonetic matches is actually IMPORTANT EVIDENCE rather than a weakness. It suggests:

1. **If BoM author accessed maps**, they did NOT rename geographic features phonetically after map place names
2. **BoM author invented distinct nomenclature**, drawing only on broader geographic CONCEPTS from maps, not specific place names
3. **Transmission pathway (if real)** worked through geographic STRUCTURE, not toponymic borrowing

**Implication**: Strong map-structure matching + zero toponymic matching = author adapted concepts but invented original place names. This is consistent with SOPHISTICATED literary composition (adapting model while creating new), not slavish copying.
