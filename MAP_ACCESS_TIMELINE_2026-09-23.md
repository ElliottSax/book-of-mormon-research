## LAHONTAN-DERIVED MAPS: CIRCULATION IN AMERICA (1703-1830)

### Publication & Reprints
- **Lahontan's *New Voyages* (1703)**:
  - 20+ French editions (1703-1763)
  - English translations: Multiple 18th-century editions
  - **RESEARCH FINDING**: British imports, NO American reprints documented 1810-1830
  - Implication: Maps would arrive as part of imported British volumes, not American reprints

### Cartographic Circulation
- **de l'Isle maps** (1718, 1720):
  - Official French cartography
  - Likely in French institutional collections
  - Less likely in American colonial/early-republic libraries
  - Periodical reproduction: Unknown

- **Moll maps** (1709-1711, 23+ editions through 1740s):
  - MOST WIDELY CIRCULATED of Lahontan-derived maps
  - British publisher (London)
  - Popular in colonial America
  - Likely in college libraries, surveyor collections, educated households
  - **Highest plausibility for American circulation**

- **Popple map** (1733, 10-sheet composite):
  - Comprehensive British colonial mapping
  - Published London
  - Specialist product (surveyors, colonial officials)
  - Less likely in general circulation than Moll

- **Senex maps** (1710s-1720s):
  - London publication
  - Copies Lahontan framework
  - Later edition, less circulation documented

### Library Holdings (Documented 1810-1830)
- **Library Company of Philadelphia**: Lahontan confirmed (by 1789)
  - Likely also held maps (Moll most probable)
  - Distance from Rigdon/Smith: 60+ miles (Philadelphia)
  - Not in immediate Rigdon's Pittsburgh access

- **Jefferson's Library**: Lahontan holdings confirmed
  - Monticello collection (Virginia)
  - Not accessible to Rigdon or Smith during composition

- **Dartmouth Library**: Lahontan documented ABSENT
  - No maps from Lahontan collection (since no Lahontan)

- **Manchester Library (near Palmyra)**: Lahontan documented ABSENT
  - Nearest library to Smith
  - No map collection evidence

### Periodical Circulation
- **North American Review** (founded 1815):
  - January 1816 article: "Voyages du Baron de la Hontan dans l'Amerique Septentrionale"
  - Likely included map illustrations (standard for geography reviews)
  - Circulated nationally by 1822 (confirmed)
  - **Possible Pittsburgh subscription** (Rigdon's period)
  - **Possible circulation via newspaper excerpt** (rural areas, periodical sharing)

### Maps as Illustrations in Publications
- **Periodicals**: Possible but not confirmed
- **Encyclopedias**: Jedidiah Morse's *American Universal Geography* (1805)
  - Widely circulated in schools
  - May have included Lahontan-influenced maps
  - **Unknown**: Whether specific cardinal-direction sea naming maps included
  
- **Newspapers**: Possible illustrations, not documented
