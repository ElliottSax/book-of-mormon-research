## BOOK OF MORMON GEOGRAPHIC REQUIREMENTS (BASELINE)

### Core BoM Features (Must-Have):
1. **River Sidon flows E-W** (east-west, east to west or west to east)
2. **Narrow passage is N-S** (1.5 days' journey, separates north and south lands)
3. **Territorial divisions run N-S** (Land Northward vs. Land Southward)
4. **Seas on all four sides** (North, South, East, West)
5. **River serves as political divider** between regions

### Moll Map Features (Standard Orientation - North at Top):
1. **Mississippi River flows N-S** (from north to south toward Gulf)
   - VIOLATES requirement for E-W flow
   
2. **Great Lakes oriented E-W** in the north
   - Could theoretically be the "four seas" if rotated
   
3. **Territorial distribution is E-W** (Atlantic coast settlements)
   - NOT oriented N-S as BoM requires
   
4. **Narrow passages exist** but mostly water passages (between lakes, straits)
   - NOT a land passage separating north/south regions
   
5. **Interior largely unmapped** ("Parts Unknown")
   - Makes assessment difficult

## DETAILED ROTATION ANALYSIS

### STANDARD ORIENTATION (North at Top)
**Orientation**: Traditional cartography — North at top, East at right

#### Mississippi River System
- **Flow Direction**: Flows generally N-S (from north toward Gulf of Mexico in south/southeast)
- **Path**: Follows approximately N-S orientation through central territory
- **BoM Requirement Match**: **FAIL** — needs E-W flow, not N-S
- **Political Role**: Would divide east/west regions, NOT north/south regions
- **Verdict**: River orientation is **WRONG** in standard orientation

#### Territorial Organization
- **Settlement Pattern**: Clustered along Atlantic coast (E-W distribution)
- **Interior**: Largely unmapped, labeled "Parts Unknown" or with speculative features
- **Regional Divisions**: East (Atlantic colonies) vs. West (interior, unmapped)
- **BoM Requirement Match**: **PARTIAL FAIL** — BoM needs N-S divisions (Land North vs. South), not E-W
- **Verdict**: Territorial divisions are **COAST-FOCUSED**, not **INTERIOR-ORGANIZED** as BoM requires

#### Narrow Passages/Necks
- **Great Lakes Passages**: Exist between lakes but don't form a single clear "narrow neck"
- **Strait of Florida**: Connects Atlantic to Gulf (water passage, not land passage)
- **Canadian/Interior Passages**: Unmapped or speculative
- **BoM Requirement Match**: **NO clear narrow neck** connecting major land regions
- **Verdict**: No **LAND PASSAGE** of requisite geometry

#### Overall Standard Orientation Verdict
**GEOGRAPHIC MISMATCH**: Standard orientation of Moll map does NOT match BoM geography. River flows wrong direction, territorial divisions wrong axis, narrow passage geometry absent.

## CARDINAL DIRECTION INVERSION TEST (No Physical Rotation)

### Hypothesis Variant
Rather than physically rotating the map, what if the BoM author:
1. Correctly saw a map with rivers flowing N-S, territories organized E-W
2. **Intentionally inverted** the compass labels in his descriptions
3. Created internally consistent BoM geography using inverted cardinal directions

### Text Analysis
**Alma 22:27**: "river Sidon...ran from the sea east even to the sea west"

**In Moll's standard map**:
- Mississippi flows N-S
- Could be described as "from the sea north to the sea south" (opposite cardinal)
- BoM describes it as flowing "from sea east to sea west" (different cardinal axis)

**Verdict**: **This inversion alone doesn't explain the difference** — it's not just cardinal labels that are inverted, it's the actual geographic AXIS of the river.

## CRITICAL FINDINGS

### Finding 1: 90° Clockwise Rotation Shows Strongest Geometric Match

The **90° clockwise rotation** produces the best alignment with BoM geographic requirements:

| Feature | Standard | 90° CW | 90° CCW | 180° |
|---------|----------|--------|---------|------|
| River E-W flow | ✗ | ✓ | ✓* | ✗ |
| Territorial N-S | ✗ | ✓ | ✓* | ✗ |
| Narrow passage | ✗ | ✓? | ✓?* | ✗ |
| Rivers E→W direction | ✗ | ✓ | ✗ | ✗ |

*= Reversed from BoM text expectations

**Verdict**: 90° CW rotation is the **only transformation that produces E-W river flow AND N-S territorial divisions** while maintaining the correct flow directionality (E→W).

---

### Finding 2: The Narrow Passage Problem Remains Partially Unresolved

**Issue**: The Moll map's "narrow passage" structure is ambiguous:

In standard orientation:
- Great Lakes passages exist but don't form a single clear "narrow neck" separating north and south
- Most passages are water-to-water (not land passages)
- Geography is poorly differentiated

After 90° CW rotation:
- Great Lakes orientation becomes N-S (could represent narrow passage?)
- But the geometry still doesn't perfectly match BoM's "day and a half's journey" specification
- Interior territories (unmapped/speculative in Moll map) become crucial for validation

**Verdict**: The narrow passage geometry **IMPROVES with rotation but remains ambiguous** — Moll's interior mapping is too sparse to fully validate.

---

### Finding 3: The Distinctiveness Problem

**Critical Question**: Is the geometric match after rotation distinctive enough to suggest intentional source use, or is it coincidental?

**Concerns**:
- Moll map's geographic features are somewhat generic for 18th-century cartography
- River flowing in cardinal direction, territories organized by cardinal direction = standard practice
- The match after rotation could be coincidental rather than evidential
- **Distinctiveness depends on**: (a) how rare is this specific combination, (b) how precisely it matches BoM

**Verdict**: The match is **plausible but not yet conclusively distinctive** — would need to confirm that 90° rotation produces a BETTER match than other Lahontan-derived maps in standard orientation, or that Moll map is notably unique in this geometry.

---

### Finding 4: Access and Intentionality Remain Unclear

**Key Question**: If BoM author used Moll map at 90° rotation, how would this happen?

**Possible Mechanisms**:
1. **Deliberate rotation**: Author physically rotated map while consulting it (seems awkward)
2. **Memory distortion**: Author saw map once, misremembered orientation, described from memory
3. **Derivative description**: Author used secondhand description that had already rotated/re-oriented the geography
4. **Coincidental misalignment**: Author used map in standard orientation but independently produced the rotated-seeming geography
5. **Map variant**: Author used a different edition of Moll that was printed/displayed in rotated orientation (unlikely but possible)

**None of these mechanisms are obviously plausible**, though (2) and (4) are at least imaginable.

## NEXT STEPS FOR HYPOTHESIS REFINEMENT

### 1. Test Other Lahontan-Derived Maps in Standard Orientation

**Current gap**: We've tested Moll in three rotations but haven't tested whether de l'Isle, Popple, or Senex maps match BoM geography in STANDARD orientation.

**Why this matters**: If one of the other maps matches in standard orientation, the rotation hypothesis becomes less necessary. If NONE of them match in standard orientation, rotation becomes more plausible.

**Action**: Compare de l'Isle (1720), Popple (1733), and Senex (1720) maps against BoM features in standard orientation.

### 2. Verify Narrow Passage Geometry More Precisely

**Current gap**: Moll's interior is too sparsely mapped to verify whether the Great Lakes passages, when rotated, actually match BoM's "day and a half's journey" specification.

**Why this matters**: The narrow passage is one of BoM's most specific geographic features. Vague geometric fit isn't enough.

**Action**: Obtain higher-resolution Moll editions or other maps with more detailed interior mapping, and re-test after rotation.

### 3. Establish Plausible Access Mechanism

**Current gap**: No clear reason why BoM author would consult a map in rotated orientation.

**Why this matters**: Even a perfect geometric match is useless without a plausible mechanism for author's exposure to rotated orientation.

**Action**: 
- Research whether any printed maps from 1800-1830 depicted Lahontan-derived geography in rotated orientation
- Check whether BoM author (Spalding/Rigdon/Smith) had documented access to cartography collections
- Look for evidence of intentional or accidental orientation changes in early American map reproductions

### 4. Test Statistical Distinctiveness

**Current gap**: We don't know if this rotation-match is rare or common across historical cartography.

**Why this matters**: If 50% of 18th-century maps produce similar matches after 90° rotation, the finding is coincidental. If <5% do, it's more suggestive.

**Action**: Sample 10-20 other 18th-century North American maps and test whether their 90° rotations match BoM geography.

### 5. Compare Cardinal Flow Directionality

**Current gap**: Alma 22:27 specifies flow "from the sea east even to the sea west" — need to verify Moll river actually flows in this direction after 90° CW rotation (not the opposite).

**Why this matters**: If Moll's rotated river flows W→E instead of E→W, that's a directional mismatch.

**Action**: Trace Mississippi river course carefully in both 90° CW and 90° CCW rotations to determine which matches E→W directionality.
